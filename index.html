About Celtic Support

The right support starts with the right connection.

Finding a support worker is about more than filling a shift. Celtic Support takes time to understand your routines, goals, preferences and the kind of person you feel comfortable having in your life.

We help participants, families and supporters explore suitable support worker options and arrange responsive support. If an NDIS review or appeal becomes part of your journey, our advocacy service can also help you understand and prepare for that process.

Frequently asked questions

Useful things to know.

How do you help me find a support worker?

We begin by learning about your needs, preferences and goals. We then help you explore possible workers and make an informed choice. The final decision about who supports you remains yours.

What does on-call support mean?

It means you can discuss responsive support for unexpected or short-notice needs. Availability, response times, costs and the exact scope of support must be agreed in advance; it is not an emergency service.

Can you guarantee a particular worker will be available?

No. Worker availability and suitability can change. We will communicate clearly about the options available, but we cannot guarantee a particular match or shift.

Are you part of the NDIA?

No. Celtic Support is an independent support business. We are not the NDIA, the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission or a government agency.

Can you guarantee the outcome of a review?

No. NDIS and Tribunal decisions are made by the relevant decision-maker. We cannot guarantee a particular outcome, but we can help you understand the process and prepare your information carefully.

Do you still provide NDIS advocacy?

Yes. Advocacy is a secondary service for participants who need help understanding an NDIS decision, preparing for a review or appeal, or organising relevant evidence. Time limits can apply, so consider seeking advice promptly.

Do you provide legal advice?

Celtic Support provides non-legal advocacy and practical support. We do not provide legal advice. If your matter needs legal advice, we may suggest that you contact a suitably qualified lawyer or community legal service.

What should I include in my first email?

A short description of your situation, the kind of help you are seeking and any important dates is enough. Please avoid sending sensitive documents until we have discussed a safe and suitable way to share them.

Can a family member or supporter contact you?

Yes. Family members, nominees and other supporters are welcome to make an enquiry. We will respect the participant’s choices, privacy and authority throughout.
